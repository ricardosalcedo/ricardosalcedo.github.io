## The Short Version

It started with Visual Basic in college. Then a summer internship my mom forced me into, where I inherited a Java layer sitting on top of COBOL. I didn't write it. I learned it, became the expert, grew it, supported it. Something clicked. Not just the code. The feeling of solving a real problem for a real person on the other side of the screen.

I spent 17 years with that same company. Not in one place. Across the globe. Project to project, country to country, bank to bank. Each stop meant new cultures, new people, new implementations. And the food. Always the food. Every country had a dish that made the distance from home worth it.

Those years gave me something no course or certification ever could: the ability to walk into any room, anywhere in the world, and figure it out. New tech stack? Learn it. New culture? Adapt. New problem nobody's solved before? Good. That's the interesting ones.

I transitioned to AWS and Amazon to expand my tech story. More settled life. More exposure to complex stacks, different customers, a company culture very different from what I'd known for nearly two decades. I'm grateful for this chapter. But I'd be lying if I said I don't miss the country hops and the friends scattered across time zones.

Now I'm looking forward. AI transforming people's lives for good. Making a wider impact. Not buried at a desk but debating, building, seeing the world while shaping what comes next.

## A Letter to Garcia

There's a famous essay called *"A Message to Garcia"* by Elbert Hubbard. It tells the story of a US lieutenant tasked with delivering a letter to General Calixto García, a Cuban rebel leader hiding somewhere in the mountains during the Spanish-American War. No address. No directions. No intel on his whereabouts. The lieutenant took the letter and made it through.

He didn't ask "which mountain?" He didn't request a committee. He figured it out.

That story has followed me through my career and through life. Give me an ambiguous destination and I'll find the path. I don't need perfect information to start moving. I don't wait for the roadmap to be complete. Ambiguity and adversity aren't blockers. They're the terrain. You move through them.

## My System

Everything feeds into a central Slack channel. Observability alerts, compliance checks, code quality scans, dependency audits, deployment status — all routed to one place where I make decisions, not chase information.

**Autonomous agents work for me around the clock:**
- Track code health across repos, submit bug fixes and improvements via PR
- Monitor production observability — metrics, logs, anomalies surface before tickets get filed
- Enforce compliance and security policies continuously, not just at review time
- Staff app-specific interest channels — answering questions, triaging issues, keeping users informed

**But production is sacred.** AI proposes, CI/CD disposes:
- Every environment gate requires passing builds, tests, security scans, and provenance verification
- No AI-generated change reaches production without full pipeline validation
- Access controls, audit trails, and signed artifacts — non-negotiable
- Agents operate freely in dev/staging; production deploys only through hardened CI/CD with human approval

The philosophy: let AI move fast everywhere it's safe, lock it down everywhere it's not.
